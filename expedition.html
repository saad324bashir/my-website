Islamabad to Skardu. Fourteen days.

The road took twenty years to build. It runs 1,300 kilometers from Islamabad through the valleys of Kashmir, over Babusar Top at 4,173 meters, north through Hunza to Khunjerab Pass at the Chinese border — the highest paved international crossing on earth — then east to Skardu at the base of K2. More than 7,000 glaciers. More than any country outside the polar regions.

On Safety

Pakistan. Let's address it directly.

Riders ask about safety. The honest answer is: it depends where you're going and what you're prepared for.

The Karakoram is a route traveled by hundreds of motorcyclists every season. The region is politically stable and openly welcoming of foreign travelers. The communities along this road have hosted visitors for decades — long before it became a route people talk about.

The real risks here are the ones you'd find on any remote mountain road: terrain, altitude, weather, mechanical failure. Those are the risks we prepare for.

What's in place

Licensed mechanic on the support vehicle for all 14 days — the same roads, the same bikes, for over a decade

English-speaking local guide with ground knowledge no GPS provides

All permits and logistics handled before departure

Travel and medical insurance required from all riders — details provided on acceptance

No one can tell you there's no risk. We can tell you what the risk actually is — and what we've done about it.

Built for the Unexpected

When things don't go
to plan.

Mechanical issues, weather shifts, altitude, and road delays are not surprises on the Karakoram. They are part of why the support vehicle, mechanic, local guide, and daily briefings exist. Here's what actually happens.

Mechanical Issue

The mechanic handles it. A licensed mechanic from Pakistan Bikers travels with us for all 14 days — the same roads, the same bikes, over a decade. You don't troubleshoot alone.

Need to Leave the Bike

The support vehicle is there. Your main bag travels in it the entire trip. If you need to come off the bike for any reason — injury, fatigue, a day off — you get in the vehicle and keep moving with the group.

Altitude Sickness

It happens. We build acclimatization into the route and watch for symptoms during daily briefings. If someone is struggling, pacing adjusts. Descent is always an option. The local guide has managed this before.

Weather or Landslide

The Karakoram moves. Landslides and road closures are part of this terrain — the local team monitors conditions daily and adjusts the route when needed. Built-in buffer days exist for exactly this reason.

Emergency Exit

If someone needs to leave the expedition early, we have a protocol. It's not comfortable to discuss before you go — but it exists, it's been thought through, and it's part of why travel insurance is required from all riders.

No one can promise nothing will go wrong. We can promise you won't face it alone.
