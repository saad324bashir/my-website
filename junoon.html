A Nomad's Dictionary: The First Entry Junoon

The divine madness of longing.

Some words don't translate. Junoon is one of them.

The Urdu is جنون. The translation is usually madness, or obsession. Neither is quite right.

It's the pull that arrives before the plan does. The thing that moved you toward something before you had a good reason — and wouldn't let you stop, even when you did.

The Sufi poets named it. Not as a problem. As the most honest signal a person has.

What it feels like

You know it by the way it moves.

In exhaustion

When you're done and still going. Not from discipline. From something deeper — a warmth that pushes you through anyway.

In momentum

When you stop asking whether you should and just move. The question answers itself. You realize you've already decided — you just haven't said it out loud yet.

In uncertainty

When you don't know what's at the other end and go anyway. Not recklessly. Deliberately. The not-knowing is the point.

Most people feel this once in a while and don't name it. Some build their lives around chasing it. You probably already know which one you are.

Why Pakistan

This place forces it out of you.

Not gently. The scale refuses to let you feel large. The road takes days to build trust. The people are immediate in a way most places aren't — hospitality so specific and unglamorous that it stops you mid-sentence.

You can't coast here. The landscape won't let you stay on autopilot. Every hour on the Karakoram is a negotiation between what you planned and what's actually in front of you.

This is not a warning. It's the reason you're going.
